The mistake · evidence

One right answer is weak evidence

40%after one explanation→71%one right answer: AI moves on→90%two in a row: reference moves on

How sure the teacher can be that the idea was learned, using the rule the AI was given (right 9 in 10 if known, 1 in 4 if not). Move on too early and the next idea clicks 5% of the time instead of 30–60%.

Times per child it moves on right after a lucky guessAI 1.4–2.0 · reference 0.5

Cost of trusting one right answer (switched off in the five rules)−8 points on a child’s exam ≈ one idea fewer of six

Whole gap between AI and reference7 points on a child’s exam

Exam score per child: no teaching 33%, a teacher who can see inside 82%, so teaching can add about 50 points at most.

This one habit is about as large as the whole gap. It is a general weakness: drawing conclusions from noisy evidence about a hidden state.

Two references, same tools as the AI

A trained policy, and the same ideas written as five rules

PPO trained

What it is: a small neural network (two layers of 128) that picks the next action.

How it was made: reinforcement learning (PPO) on about half a million simulated children with random seeds, never the test children. Reward: the exam score.

What it sees: only what the AI sees, its own actions and the child’s answers. Never the hidden state.

Role: the official reference the judges rerun in Harbor.

0.73 on 1,200 fresh children

5 rules written down

What it is: a short rule list a person could follow with pen and paper.

How it was made: we watched what PPO does and wrote its habits as five rules; six numbers in them (like “two right answers”) were set on training children.

What it sees: the same as the AI and PPO.

Role: shows that the winning decisions are not a black box: the AI could have made them.

0.67 on the same 1,200 children

For comparison: frontier AI agents score about 0.55, a textbook rulebook from the learning-science literature 0.56.

The five rules, in priority order

Each rule is tied to a mechanism

1 · Fix a wrong rule as soon as its typical answer appearsfix works, re-explaining rarely

2 · Teach once, then quiz; move on only after two right in a rowturns 71% into 90% sure

3 · At most two explanations per idea per session; still stuck? quiz the idea before itrepeats work less; no prerequisite, 5%

4 · On Monday, quiz before teachingthe child may already know it

5 · No routine review quizzes at the start of a sessionkeep working on the next idea

The five rules quiz as often as the AI (67% vs 68% of turns) and still win clearly. Quizzing a lot is not the problem; what each does with the answers is.
